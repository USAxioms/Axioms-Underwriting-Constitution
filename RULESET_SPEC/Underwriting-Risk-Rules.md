# Underwriting Risk Rules

## Purpose
Defines the constitutional, deterministic framework for evaluating underwriting
risk across all supported domains. Risk scoring must be transparent, auditable,
and free from discretionary or model-driven variability.

## Deterministic Risk Scoring
Risk scores must be computed using fixed constitutional formulas. Identical
inputs must always produce identical outputs.

## Required Inputs
- Exposure profile
- Domain-specific risk factors
- Temporal risk indicators
- Behavioral risk indicators
- Stability and solvency metrics
- Compliance status

## Risk Components
- Exposure Risk: magnitude and duration of exposure
- Domain Risk: inherent risk of the underwriting domain
- Temporal Risk: time-based volatility or uncertainty
- Behavioral Risk: applicant behavior patterns and reliability
- Systemic Risk: cross-domain or environmental factors

## Constitutional Constraints
Risk scoring must:
- Use transparent formulas
- Avoid opaque statistical models
- Exclude discretionary overrides
- Maintain regulator alignment
- Preserve fairness and consistency

## Safety Layer Requirements
Risk evaluation must pass through the constitutional safety layer to:
- Validate threshold integrity
- Prevent bias or manipulation
- Ensure cross-domain consistency

## Auditability
All risk evaluations must be immutably logged in the cognitive ledger, including:
- Input set
- Component-level calculations
- Ruleset version
- Safety-layer validations
- Final risk score
